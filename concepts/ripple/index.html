Lane 1 2026 to present

Splashery

An interactive Gaussian splat playground that runs entirely in the browser. Pick a toy (a real captured object, or one generated from a seed), spin it, poke it, blow on it, splash paint on it, drop it, watch it fall apart and come back, make your own, and share it as a link, an embed, a GIF or a video.

Two of its science toys are instruments. The ripple tank solves the 2D wave equation on a grid and draws the water as a surface of splats: one or two dippers, a plane wave, one or two slits, with the frequency and the speed live, and a tap drops a pebble. The light bench traces rays with Snell's law through parts you drag: lenses, mirrors, a BK7 prism, a glass block and a fiber, with the numbers beside them. Its lens faces are real circles, so a lens keeps its real aberrations.

The water at the top of this page is a small tank on the same equation. Beside this text is a small bench with one BK7 prism, the glass's index taken from its Sellmeier fit. Turn it and the colors fan out or fold back inside the glass.

  • Light lab. Line spectra of sixteen elements from NIST data, a prism and a grating (d sin θ = m λ).
  • Sound. A Chladni plate whose sand moves live with the sound.
  • Every splat, every frame. Effects run on the GPU: poke, wind, dissolve, drop, magnet, twist, slice, paint.
  • No build step. Static files and ES modules, on the PlayCanvas engine, WebGPU with a WebGL2 fallback.

Ryan defined the project's vision and its toy categories, refined the interaction design through hands-on testing, and contributed mathematical recipes.

Lane 2 2026 to present

grooph

Visual loop graphs for coding-agent workflows. You draw who builds, who checks, where a person decides and when the work stops.

grooph checks that every loop names a stop, then compiles the graph into a prompt package for a separate coding-agent harness. It runs no agent and calls no model, and graphs stay on your device.
