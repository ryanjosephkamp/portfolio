01 Gaussian splats

        
Splashery

        
An interactive Gaussian splat playground.

        
A toy box of 3D Gaussian splats that runs in the browser. Pick a toy, then poke it, blow wind through it, dissolve it, drop it, pull it with a magnet, twist it, slice it or paint it: effects that run on the GPU for every splat, every frame.

        
I defined the project vision and toy categories, directed multi-agent development, and refined the interaction design through hands-on testing.

        
Its science shelves hold a virus and a neuron, electron orbitals and proteins, a brain, an eye, and a maths shelf with a Lorenz attractor, a Klein bottle and a gyroid. Those three appear on this page too, redrawn by a far smaller renderer written for it.

        
PlayCanvas · static files and ES modules · no build step, no framework, no server

02 Procedural form

        
Recipes

        
A toy that is written, not scanned.

        
Some of Splashery’s toys are captured: real scans, up to 450,000 splats. The rest are generated, built from seeded recipes. I developed and contributed selected mathematical recipes to the playground.

        
Its Tinkerer’s Manual covers what one splat is (a Gaussian and its covariance), how a recipe is built, and a splat equation language.

        
Every form on this page is made the same way in miniature. A seeded recipe a few lines long says where each splat goes, which way its stroke runs, and which ink it takes. The card at the top of the page lets you change one, or write your own.

03 Visualization

        
The speed–accuracy trade-off

        
Undergraduate research capstone, April 2025.

        
Every streak in the plot below is one decision being made. Evidence drifts toward the upper line, the right answer, while noise shoves it around, and the decision happens the moment a streak touches a line. Pull the lines apart and every answer is slower but more often right. Push them together and the answers come fast and sloppy.

        
        
The splat figure for this section is the same model in the page’s own material: thirty streams of evidence, each with its own fixed noise. Move the line or the dial and those same thirty are replayed under the new rule. Teal streams ended on the right answer and orange ones on the wrong one. A stream that reaches the edge still undecided is printed in both inks.

        
The capstone, The Speed-Accuracy Trade-Off in Perceptual Decision-Making, modeled evidence accumulation as a diffusion process and used dynamic programming and value iteration to study decision boundaries in a simulated autonomous-vehicle obstacle-avoidance scenario. It was advised by Dr. Hyunjoong Kim.

04 Graphs for agents

        
grooph

        
Visual loop graphs for coding-agent workflows.

        
Draw who builds, who checks, where a person decides and when the work stops. The tool validates graph documents and compiles prompt packages for a separate coding-agent harness.

        
I implemented a multi-agent pipeline and refined the visual tool for expressing builder and checker roles, human decision points, and stopping conditions.
