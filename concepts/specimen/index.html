I’m a master’s student in computer science at the University of Cincinnati, with degrees in mathematics and neuroscience from the same place. What I want to make is splats that explain: pictures of science you can take apart until the thing makes sense.

The cell on the slide is the smallest example I could build. Turn the focus down and its 4,096 Gaussians merge, pair by pair, until the whole neuron is one soft blob with the same mass, center and spread it started with. Turn it back up and tap: the spike you see is the Hodgkin–Huxley equations, solved while you watch.

Case II the method

How to read the slide

Four things are going on in the specimen at the top of this page. None of them is decoration.

  1. One splat

    A Gaussian: a center, a spread in three directions (its covariance Σ), a weight and a stain. Turn it by a rotation R and its shadow on the screen is a Gaussian again, with spread RΣRT. That is how every blob here is drawn, from every side.

  2. Focus

    Lowering the focus pairs splats up. Each pair becomes the one Gaussian with the same mass, center and covariance, so level 0 is the whole cell as a single splat and no level in between has gained or lost anything. A script checks all 4,095 merges against the splats beneath them, and all 13 levels against the finest. The largest disagreement is about 10−15.

  3. Light

    These splats don’t glow. They absorb, the way a stained section does on a bright stage: each takes a little light out, the losses add, and what is left is e to the minus sum. Their order never matters, so nothing on this page is sorted.

  4. Spike

    Fire integrates the Hodgkin–Huxley equations, with the 1952 squid-axon constants, from rest after one millisecond of current. The chart is that solution. The color that runs down the axon is the same solution, delayed by distance, pausing at each gap in the myelin.
